# Local business sites

Barbers, cafés, salons, trades, gyms, restaurants, studios.

**Success:** a local customer, usually on their phone, knows within seconds what you do, roughly what it costs, where you are, when you're open, and how to book or call. The site feels like *this* place, not the category.

**Starting dials:** layout variance 6, motion 4, density 4.

## The template to avoid

Every category has its cliché, and plain AI output falls straight into it:
- the dark, moody barbershop with gold accents and a barber-pole stripe
- the rustic bakery in kraft brown with a script font
- the spa with sage green and a lotus
- the gym in black and neon

Also avoid stock photos, a gallery of ten empty boxes, and invented reviews.

## Build it with

- **The place's own personality:** the concept spine comes from this business specifically. Its street, its history, its craft, its regulars, its sense of humour. Use local references with care, not as decoration.
- **Above the fold on mobile:** what you do, where, and the main action (Book, Call, Order). Phone numbers are tap-to-call links.
- **Prices listed plainly.** People want to know before they book. If the brief has no prices, use clearly marked example prices and flag them.
- **Hours, address and directions** easy to find: today's hours and an open/closed state are a nice alive detail. Link to a map rather than embedding a heavy one.
- **Booking that actually works** on a phone: a real flow (service → time → details → confirmation) or a clear link to the booking provider.
- **Photos:** the place and the work matter most here, but you can't supply them. Use a few intentional photo slots and an illustrated signature visual (see `craft/images.md`), not a wall of placeholders.

## Checks

- The main action is visible without scrolling at 390×844.
- Today's hours, the address and the phone number are reachable within one scroll on mobile.
- No more than three photo placeholders visible on any one screen.
