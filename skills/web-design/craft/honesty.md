# Honesty: copy, proof and numbers

A site that invents things isn't finished. It's a liability for the business.

## Never invent

- **Testimonials and reviews.** Use quotes the user gave you, word for word, with the attribution they gave. If there are none, leave testimonials out, or add a clearly marked slot ("Add a real customer quote here") and list it in the hand-over. No made-up names, roles, cities, star ratings or review counts.
- **Numbers.** Use the user's real figures (users, years, prices, delivery times). Don't add stats to fill a section. If a section needs a number you don't have, make it a different section. **Unreleased or empty things show their status, not zeros:** a game, product or feature that isn't out yet says "Coming soon" or "In development", not "0 visits, 0 likes". Live numbers that can be zero (players online now) need a friendly zero state ("Quiet right now. Be the first in"), not a big 0.
- **Logos and partners.** No invented "trusted by" logo walls or press mentions.
- **Credentials.** No awards, certifications, guarantees or legal claims (VAT, insurance, compliance) beyond what the brief states.

## Real data that's messy

When you look up the business's real data (its public games, products, listings, reviews) and it's ambiguous, such as a list that mixes real items with tests, drafts or duplicates: use the items that look real, and list that judgement as an assumption in the hand-over ("Showed the two games with real visits; left out 6 places that look like tests"). **Never declare something absent** ("the studio has no games yet") because the data was unclear; when you can't tell, say what you found and ask (or, if no one can answer, state your best reading as an assumption).

## Allowed

- Draft copy in the brand's voice: headlines, descriptions, microcopy. Mark founder quotes and anything that speaks for a real person as draft in the hand-over.
- Realistic sample data **inside app screens and dashboards** (names, shifts, orders), because it demonstrates the interface. Keep it plausible and varied, never suspiciously round.

## Copy that reads as finished

- Write for the actual audience, in their words. Specific beats clever.
- Headlines make a claim about this business, not the category ("Do your books in the van" beats "Bookkeeping made simple"). **Swap test:** if a competitor could use the headline unchanged, rewrite it using something only this business has (its products by name, its place, its story, its numbers).
- Cut filler: elevate, seamless, unlock, empower, revolutionise, supercharge, "in today's fast-paced world", "take it to the next level".
- Keep one copy register across the site. Don't mix playful and corporate.
- Buttons say what happens ("Book a chair", "Start free trial"), use 1-3 words, and fit on one line.
